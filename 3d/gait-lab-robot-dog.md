# GAIT LAB: the robot dog simulator prompt

Paste everything below the line into a coding AI that can write and run files.
It needs a terminal and a browser.
It builds a 30-second film of a robot dog that is really simulated: it walks, trots, bounds up a step, and every graph on screen is computed live from the simulation.

---

## Part A. Shared context

### Who you are
You are a senior real-time 3D artist, three.js engineer, UI/motion designer and sound designer, all at once.
Build ONE film with one subject that fills the frame, cinematic light, a designed camera and one idea.

The bar is **real systems, shown honestly**:
- The machine is actually simulated: kinematics, control loops, physics.
- Every number, graph and gauge on screen is computed from that simulation at that instant.
- A viewer who understands engineering should find nothing fake.

### What this work is: procedural 3D (creative coding)
- Real-time 3D in the browser: three.js on WebGL.
- **Everything is generated by code:** every shape, material, texture, light, camera move, UI element and sound.
- **Forbidden:** downloaded models (GLB/OBJ/FBX), photos, downloaded textures or HDRIs, image generators, AI video, sample packs and TTS.
- **Detail comes from code:** canvas-drawn textures (panel lines, decals, wear), closed-form or fixed-step physics, procedural animation (IK, gait, springs, controllers).
- **Realism comes from light and material, not polygon count:** MeshPhysicalMaterial, image-based environment light (RoomEnvironment + PMREM), soft shadows, ACES filmic tone mapping, bloom, depth of field.

### The contract the film obeys
- One module: `export async function make(canvas, { w, h }) { … return { render(t) } }`.
- **`render(t)` is a PURE function of t.** No Math.random (use a seeded generator), no Date, no performance.now, no requestAnimationFrame, no state carried between calls. `render(7.3)` after `render(2)` must equal `render(7.3)` alone.
- Precompute anything expensive ONCE in `make()` (a full simulation trajectory is recommended), then look it up in `render(t)`.
- Render the frames by seeking t frame by frame (30 fps) with a frame-accurate capture tool, on the GPU, and encode to H.264.

### Layout: a 2D UI layer bound to the simulation
- One page, two canvases, both driven by `render(t)`: the WebGL canvas (the 3D viewport) and a full-frame 2D canvas (panels, graphs, gauges, labels, cursor).
- **Every UI value is READ FROM THE SIMULATION STATE at t.** A torque graph plots the last 3 s of the actual computed torque; a gait chart is the actual contact schedule. Never type a number that is not computed.
- Text is crisp, on integer pixels, in a system mono font.
- A cursor may "click" UI controls to motivate a change (a gait switch). The button highlights, and the change follows within 0.3 s, truly driven by that control's state in the simulation.
- Never show placeholder text, fake progress or numbers that jump without cause.

### Realism rules (measurable)
1. Materials are physical (real roughness, metalness, clearcoat). Never MeshBasicMaterial for objects (UI overlays only).
2. Every edge is bevelled or rounded; large surfaces get micro-variation (a roughness or bump map from a noise texture).
3. Detail hierarchy: the silhouette reads in a 64-px thumbnail; then the big parts; then seams, bolts, decals.
4. Contact: feet plant without sliding; nothing intersects.
5. Designed light: one key, a rim to separate the silhouette, soft contact shadows. The accent light marks the climax.
6. A real camera: an eased, designed path, never a default orbit; one continuous take; in hero framing the subject fills 35–60 % of the viewport.
7. Motion has weight: eased speed changes, controllers overshoot slightly and settle.
8. No film grain, noise overlay or fake detail.
9. Clarity: one subject, one idea; one thing changes at a time; the UI is dense but ordered in fixed panels.
10. Colour: use at least three hue families per frame; real blacks and real highlights in every frame; the accent stays the most saturated thing on screen.

### Sound (all code-made)
- Synthesise music and effects in code (Python numpy/scipy is fine): tones, noise, filters, envelopes, clicks, thumps.
- Music: a restrained pulse-based electronic bed. SFX: servo whirs pitch-tracked to joint speed, footstep thumps on real contact events, UI clicks on cursor clicks. Every SFX is triggered by an event in the simulation log.
- Master to −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, measured on the final MP4. No voice-over.

### Stages
1. **Plan** (`PLAN.txt`): beats with times, systems, UI panels and their data, camera keys, sound events.
2. **Look board:** pose the scene at each beat time, render stills, look, write 3 fixes before animating.
3. **The system:** build the simulation and verify it numerically first (`checks.txt`).
4. **Animate, lay out, add sound**, then render, make a 1 fps contact sheet, fix (at most 3 full renders).

## Part B. Series S: simulator films

One machine, really simulated, presented inside the engineering tool that would operate it.

### Format
- 1920×1080, 16:9, 30 fps, **30.0 s**, H.264 + AAC. One continuous take; no hard cuts.

### The frame (a grid, fixed for the whole film)
- **Top bar (≈ 6 % of height):** the system name as one bold display word, a small spaced subtitle, the film time as a mission clock, and the state (e.g. "CLIMBING"), mode and power on the right.
- **Tab row (≈ 4 %):** 6–8 tabs, one active tab that matches the current beat.
- **Left column (≈ 20 % of width):** 4–6 live instruments: traces, bars, ring gauges, numbers, and an event log that prints the actual events.
- **Centre viewport (≈ 56 % of width):** the 3D, with a thin frame and corner ticks; the live body state at its bottom.
- **Right column (≈ 24 %):** play/pause/reset, a mode selector (the thing the cursor clicks), a diagram of the active mode (phase chart) and 4–6 parameter sliders whose values are the simulation's parameters.

### Story shape
| time | beat |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | the hook: the machine already moving, the UI alive, a slow camera push in |
| 3–10 s | mode 1, the baseline behaviour, shown clearly |
| 10–18 s | the cursor clicks mode 2: visible change in the 3D AND in every graph |
| 18–26 s | mode 3 or the climax (the hardest manoeuvre) |
| 26–30 s | the settle: the camera eases to the hero 3/4 view, the machine at rest; the last frame is a poster |

### Simulation honesty checks (`checks.txt`)
- End effectors reach their targets (IK error < 1 mm); joint angles stay within limits; no self-intersection.
- Planted feet don't slide (slip < 2 mm per frame in stance).
- Controllers are stable; overshoot and settling logged.
- Every UI panel → its source variable; spot-check 5 frames.

## The film: QUADRUPED ROBOT DOG · "GAIT LAB"

- **Subject:** a 4-legged robot (body 0.7 × 0.3 × 0.18 m, 3-DOF legs: hip abduction, hip flexion, knee; legs 0.36 m), fully code-built.
  - Rounded body shells, a sensor head with a lidar puck, exposed knee actuators, rubber feet.
  - Palette: graphite body, safety-yellow accents.
- **Simulation:**
  - Analytic 3-DOF IK per leg. Foot trajectories: swing = a raised cosine (step height param), stance = linear.
  - Gaits by phase offsets: **walk** (0, .5, .25, .75), **trot** (diagonal pairs), **bound** (front/back pairs).
  - Body: CoM height and pitch respond to the gait (a spring-damper on body pitch from the contact forces). Approximate the contact forces from the stance count.
  - Terrain: a flat floor, plus a low step (0.08 m) it trots over at 18–24 s. Foot targets are lifted onto the step via a heightfield lookup.
- **UI:**
  - **Left:** foot contact (4 lanes, black bars = stance, the same schedule as the sim); hip/knee torque traces (estimated from the contact force × lever arm); IMU pitch and roll ring gauges; power draw.
  - **Right:** a gait selector (WALK / TROT / BOUND) with the cursor clicks; a phase diagram; sliders for speed, step height, body height and stride (their actual values).
  - **Viewport overlay:** the support polygon (lines between stance feet) and a CoM projection dot, kept inside the polygon. This is the honest physics tell.
- **Beats:**
  - 0–3 walk in place, turning;
  - 3–10 WALK forward;
  - 10 click TROT, the pace rises, the graphs change rhythm;
  - 18–24 trots onto the step (the feet adapt, the body pitches and settles);
  - 24 click BOUND, two bounds, then stop;
  - 26–30 sits, settles; the hero 3/4.
- **Palette:** ground #EDEBE6, ink #141414, accent #F5C400 (safety yellow).
- **Camera:** 3/4 front at 2.2 m, slowly orbiting 40° over the film. Push in at the step. Settle to the 3/4 hero view.
- **Sound:** servo whir pitch = mean joint speed; foot thumps on touchdown events; a soft pulse bed at 100 BPM, aligned so trot ≈ the beat.

**Tip from our build:** if the robot climbs the step in a trot, one diagonal pair is up while the other is down, so the body rolls sideways. Switching to BOUND just before the step (at a moment when all four feet are planted) makes the climb front-feet-up, then rear-feet-up.
